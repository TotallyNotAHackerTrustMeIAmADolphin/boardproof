# Architecture

**This file is the decision record, not the current overview.** For a ten-minute picture of what the project is and how it works, read [docs/OVERVIEW.md](OVERVIEW.md) first; the glossary is [docs/CONTEXT.md](CONTEXT.md). Come back here for the reasoning behind a design choice and for the known facts and gotchas with their evidence. Entries are dated and some are historical: where a later decision replaced an earlier one, the entry says so. "Build ticket N", "ticket N" and "protocol N" are explained in docs/CONTEXT.md.

This file records the current design, the decisions behind it, and the known facts and gotchas with their evidence. It was rewritten for the 2026-10-11 pivot. The full history (sprint logs, investigation narratives, the September prior-art survey) was in git at commit `3380b34` (the backlog, the research notes, older versions of this file), which belongs to the maintainers' archive and may not exist in a public copy. Ticket IDs such as P4-07 refer to that commit's backlog; they are historical references.

## Product shape (pivot, 2026-10-11)

- **Tiered promise by board class** (terms are defined in docs/CONTEXT.md). The **Autonomous tier** covers board classes below a measured complexity ceiling. Above that ceiling is the **Copilot tier**: the human owns power routing and zone topology, and the agent assists with schematic, placement, signal routing and verification. Copilot is the default. A board class becomes autonomous only once it passes the acceptance gate.
- **Acceptance gate**: only a `verify_board` PASS may call a board routed or done. docs/TESTING.md defines it.
- **Acceptance ladder**: rung 1 is the USB-C ATtiny1616 breakout, rung 2 a mid-size signal-only 2-layer board (about 30 nets), rung 3 openMPPT. The first rung that fails marks the autonomous ceiling. docs/ROADMAP.md has the details.
- **Why**: the external evidence (PCBWorld benchmark, arXiv 2607.05915, summarised in the maintainers' research notes) shows clean autonomous routing only for signal-only 2-4 layer boards of up to about 40 nets / 100 pads. In that benchmark, "routed" and "clean" diverge: Freerouting reaches routability 1.00 but a Clean Pass rate of only 0.78. Every LLM agent scores 0.00 Clean Pass at 5-42 nets. openMPPT (68 multi-pad nets, 488 pads, 7 zones, a 20 A class) is outside the evidenced envelope. This project's own month on openMPPT ended without a clean route.

## Decision history (2026-10-11 and 2026-10-19)

The design changed twice by review: the paradigm review of the 2026-10-11 pivot, and a second review (`paradigm-review-2`, 2026-10-19; its tickets are cited by number) after a study of mixelpixx/KiCAD-MCP-Server (v2.8.2) and the first rung measurements. Each 2026-10-19 verdict was decided with the owner in a grilling. Fixed inputs, not reopened: the acceptance gate, the tiered promise, the acceptance ladder, the openMPPT freeze and the hard-rule-2 cross-check. The resulting design is stated once, under "Decisions"; this section keeps only the reasons and evidence that are not stated there. The build work is in the numbered build tickets (docs/CONTEXT.md).

**2026-10-11 (pivot)**
- **Batch tools over one-item-per-call: kept.** The best LLM mode in PCBWorld (interactive with DRC feedback) is a loop of batch calls.
- **Two operating modes, live IPC and headless file: partly unsupported.** Only the 9 `copilot` tools go through `resolve_backend()`; the other 10 toolsets are file-only; live schematic reads are broken in kipy 0.8.0. Copilot became a **tier** (a promise), separate from the **adapter mode** (IPC vs file); the Copilot tier does not need live IPC, so file-drift detection (designed, never built, P1-19) is a prerequisite for it (decision 12).
- **`kicad-tools`: kept for file I/O and courtyard geometry, demoted elsewhere.** It is MIT and covers schematic, PCB and footprint generation, so there is no viable non-GPL alternative, and it has produced more than 15 silent bugs here (see "Known facts and gotchas"). It is never a verification authority (`kicad-cli` is) and its DSN/SES export is not the main routing path. Replaced in part on 2026-10-19 (decisions 1 and 3).
- **Upstream contribution:** not practised in six weeks (no upstream issue or PR filed, at least eight queued). Replaced on 2026-10-19 (below).
- **Geometry over vision: kept, sharpened.** The false "done" on openMPPT came from *sampled* geometry and was caught by the user by eye; the mounting-hole false alarm came from a wrong convenience property. Geometry means `kicad-cli` output and raw S-expressions, read as complete lists; a render for human review is encouraged but never evidence.
- **Narrow human-in-the-loop: replaced.** "A human reviews each autorouted proposal" did not stop a false done claim: the checkpoint existed and the claim still came from incomplete evidence. The machine-checked gate decides done; board class decides who routes power and zones; the dry-run plus revision-token pattern stays for any destructive mutation (decision 4).
- **kiutils (GPLv3) licensing: closed** (since 2026-09-02); `kicad-tools` covers schematic I/O under MIT.
- **Phased roadmap (Phases 0-7): wrong measure.** Phases 0-6 were all marked done, yet no board was ever finished. Replaced by the acceptance ladder (docs/TESTING.md).
- **Diagnose-and-retry routing loop graded by real DRC: grading kept, loop questioned.** Both routers' self-scores anti-correlate with DRC, but on openMPPT one attempt took the whole 90-minute budget, so the retries never ran. Retries stay bounded by wall clock; rung results decide whether the loop earns its complexity.

**2026-10-19 (paradigm review v2)**

- `sourcing` (13) and `library` (3).

**Not built:** a general zone tool (rest of P3-07), track or via removal as a tool (P3-12), the general drift guard (build ticket 40) and the wiring of the lock-file guard (build ticket 41).

## Decisions

1. **File I/O: `kicad_tools` (MIT, PyPI) parses and serialises, our own write layer mutates (re-decided 2026-10-19, `paradigm-review-2` ticket 06).**
   - Parsing and serialising use `kicad_tools`' low-level API (`Document`/`SExp`), which round-trips files KiCad-equivalent. Its generators (parametric footprints, courtyard geometry) stay in use.
   - Every mutation goes through a thin write layer of our own on that API. It handles quoting, `\r` escaping, number precision and pad angles on rotation. It saves through its own temporary sibling file, the tool's self-check (decision 4) and a per-file `kicad-cli ... upgrade --force` parse check, then `os.replace`. The high-level models' setters and `PCB.save` are not used for mutation.
   - The high-level models are allowed only for reads (under the hard-rule-2 cross-check) and generators. The one high-level write allowed is building test fixtures under `tests/` (docs/TESTING.md). Geometry readers that evaluate raw tokens in the right frame, proven against `kicad-cli` position and IPC-D-356 exports, become the sanctioned source for position facts (build ticket 43).
   - **The write layer exists (build ticket 42): `kicad_mcp.write_layer`.** `save(root, path)` (any `.kicad_pcb`/`.kicad_sch`/`.kicad_mod`/`.kicad_sym` tree), `save_pcb(board, path)` (a `kicad_tools` `PCB`, replaces `board.save(path)`) and `save_project(data, path)` (`.kicad_pro` JSON) serialise with our own serialiser, write a temporary sibling in UTF-8, check it (`kicad-cli <pcb|sch|fp|sym> upgrade --force` on a scratch copy, any non-zero exit or crash code is a failure, every sub-sheet of a schematic checked on its own; a JSON read-back for the project), run an optional `verify(temp)` hook (where build ticket 22's verify-before-replace plugs in), then `os.replace`, retrying Windows' transient sharing denial (WinError 5/32) for up to 3 s with `commit_directory`'s schedule; the checks run once, before the first move (build ticket 78). On any failure the target is byte-identical. A check failure raises `WriteLayerError(code, ...)`, which names it; a failed move raises `os.replace`'s own OSError unchanged after the retry. The temporary file is removed with the same retry; if it cannot be removed, the error carries a note naming it. Typed atoms: `string`/`symbol`/`number` and `set_string`/`set_symbol`/`set_number` replace `SExp.set_value`. `load(path)` parses without newline translation. All 21 `PCB.save` sites and both `save_project` sites use it; a mutation with no MCP tool goes through the layer's typed setters and `save`, never through the high-level setters, `PCB.save` or raw text, and is reloaded and compared with the intended change. `commit_directory(temp_dir, target)` moves a verified revision directory into place (the six checkpoint tools that build one), retrying Windows' transient sharing denial for up to 3 s.
   - Always the newest `kicad_tools` release: 0.24.0 since build ticket 71 (0.23.0 from build ticket 42, adopted together with the layer's own save because 0.22.0+'s `PCB.save` and `save_project` fail on Windows). `pyproject.toml` carries only a floor (`>=0.24.0`). What each version changed for us is under "Known facts and gotchas" (`kicad_tools`), pinned per version by `tests/test_kicad_tools_facts.py`. 0.24.0's `PCB.save` works on Windows again and refuses edits it cannot persist; the layer stays the only save path all the same, because `PCB.save` writes LF where KiCad writes CRLF on Windows, writes `\r` raw, adds a top-level net table to KiCad 10 boards, and has no parse check, self-check hook or verified `os.replace` (build ticket 71).
   - Migration is incremental, not a big-bang rewrite (build tickets 38, 39, 42-44). By 2026-10-29 the write layer carries nearly every save (21 `PCB.save` sites moved in build ticket 42; 3 high-level saves remain in `tests/ratchets.toml`, together with a shrink-only list of raw writes). The losses of the old high-level path are listed under "Known facts and gotchas".
   - Never import `kiutils` (GPLv3).
2. **`kicad-cli` is the only verification authority.** That covers DRC/ERC JSON (`--format json --severity-all`), netlists and exports. Call `kicad_cli.ensure_kicad_cli_on_path()` before any `kicad_tools` call that shells out.
3. **Router backend = thin adapter; no fixed lead; the gate picks the winner per board. This supersedes the earlier "KRT leads, Freerouting cross-checks" (re-decided 2026-10-19, `paradigm-review-2` tickets 05 and 17).** KiCadRoutingTools (KRT, drandyhaas, MIT) and Freerouting (GPLv3, subprocess only) run as equals.
   - **Ranking:** set only after a fair re-measurement: Freerouting with a correct DSN, and the same rules for both, including the under-width check (build tickets 36, 46, 47). Build ticket 47 ran it under protocol 5 and could not rank them (0 of 20 attempts passed). The first official protocol-6 batches on rung 1 (2026-10-28) gave Freerouting 5 of 5 `PASS (necked)` and KRT 0 of 5, the latter from its parser reading zero pads of footprints with unquoted pad names (build ticket 112); rung 2 cannot pass for either (build ticket 106). After that the ROADMAP rule applies: a backend that is never the one passing on any rung earns no more integration work.
   - **KRT feature work is paused** until that re-measurement. Only KRT work that blocks a measurement goes ahead (the under-width delivery, build ticket 37; the 0.23.1 snapshot, build ticket 33). The owner's goal that every KRT capability is used resumes once KRT is the router that passes on a rung.
   - **Freerouting's DSN path (ticket 17):** KiCad's own Specctra exporter and SES importer (`pcbnew.ExportSpecctraDSN`, `ImportSpecctraSES`), run in KiCad's bundled Python on a scratch copy of the board. `pcbnew` only ever writes the scratch copy. The routed result reaches the real board through the write layer (decision 1), by the same delta path KRT uses, with the self-check (decision 4) and the drift guard (decision 12). The import is graded by `verify_board`, never by its return value (it returns `True` even when it skipped items), and skipped-item counts are reported. Our `kicad_tools` DSN export, its text patches, our SES parser and the y-convention code are removed (build ticket 46). With KiCad 11, which removes SWIG, the path moves to `kicad-cli pcb export specctra` plus IPC `ImportSpecctraSession`; that becomes a build ticket once KiCad 11 is released.
   - The current KRT integration rules came out of real failures. Default to `--escalation board --no-fix-drc-settings`. Pin floors with a generated `--fab-overrides` (strictest class, `max()`). Copy the sibling `.kicad_pro`, `.kicad_prl`, `.kicad_dru` and lib tables into every trial directory. Never adopt KRT's rewritten `.kicad_pro`; restore the original before grading. Apply results as a delta through the write layer (decision 1), never through KRT's writer. Exclude high-fanout power and GND nets from routing and pour them instead. Route before pouring.
   - **Feature inventory:** the KRT feature inventory in the maintainers' research notes (2026-10-11, from a read-only clone, v0.23.0). It is the checklist for "every KRT capability is used", paused together with new KRT feature work (above). Reconcile it with the earlier `krt-capability-suite` survey (its tickets 01-04, 09). The tool list is generated from `krt_registry.py --json` (71 tools), with `krt_capabilities.py --require` pinning the version, so no feature is silently forgotten.
   - **Integration mode (decided 2026-10-11): CLI subprocess.** Spawn each tool with an `argv` list (never a shell string: every KiCad net name starts with `/`, which MSYS2 rewrites). Read the `--json` / `--json-out` outputs, and treat each output file as a new board revision, copied together with its sibling project files (`copy_board.py`). Use the native `--preview` / `--dry-run` for proposals. Do not import KRT as a library.
   - **Features most easily overlooked** (details in the inventory §5): `--layer-costs` for poured planes, `--force-reroute` / `--rip-existing-nets`, `--fab-tier` and `--strict-sizes`, `--keep-away`, the design brief and `check_floorplan.py --emit-intent` with `--intent` on every placement tool, `add_rule_area.py`, `place_fanout_clearance.py`, `--write-fill` / `fill_for_delivery.py`, `--schematic-dir`, `--preview` / `--undo` / `--group-by sheet`.
4. **Checkpoint mechanism.** Every mutation that is destructive, or whose result is not yet verified, follows propose, then a `revision_token`, then confirm, with bounded and TTL-limited pending state. It is implemented for component match, autoplacement, routing, placement optimisation and graphic removal. `register_library_asset` deliberately writes without a checkpoint, because it is deterministic; the self-check rule below still applies to it.
   - **Self-check (decided 2026-10-19, `paradigm-review-2` ticket 08).** Every tool that writes a KiCad file, checkpointed or not, verifies before it replaces: it writes a temporary sibling, reloads it and compares it with the intended change, and replaces the original with `os.replace` only on a match. On a mismatch the original is untouched, the temporary file is deleted, and the tool returns `ok: false` with code `write_not_verified` and a list of mismatches (expected, actual, file location). A passed self-check means only "the write landed"; it is never evidence of a finished board. Every self-check has a test that feeds it a corrupted write and shows it fires. Ratchet tests hold the line: a ban on pad `size`/`drill`/`layers` assignment, and shrink-only lists of raw writes, convenience-property reads on mutation paths and writing tools without a self-check. The mechanism lives once, in the shared checkpoint helper (build ticket 22); the rest is build tickets 29-32.
   - The checkpoint revision token carries the project's last-known composite hash (decision 12), which confirm and the drift guard check, plus a per-proposal id, so two proposals on an unchanged project do not collide. Built in `checkpoint.py` (build ticket 22: `Pending` issues `<project hash>-<proposal id>` and the confirm's drift guard refuses a changed project; five modules use it); the drift guard for non-checkpointed writes is still build ticket 40.
5. **Adapter modes.** `resolve_backend(mode)` returns either `IpcBackend` (live, so state is always fresh) or `FileBackend`. In `auto` mode it falls back to file *reads* only when the transport is unreachable, and it fails closed for mutations. File-drift detection is specified in decision 12 and **not built yet** (build ticket 40).
6. **External tools stay at arm's length.** Each is discovered in the same order: environment variable, then PATH, then a platform glob. A missing tool returns `status: incomplete` with install instructions, never a wrong answer. Licences were checked against each tool's LICENSE file: Freerouting is GPLv3 (subprocess), easyeda2kicad AGPL-3.0 (subprocess, optional `[assets]` extra), KiKit MIT (but it needs `pcbnew`, so it runs in KiCad's bundled Python as a subprocess and stays out of `pyproject.toml`), ngspice modified BSD (subprocess, use `ngspice_con.exe`), kipy MIT (imported), KRT MIT (subprocess), and the CDFER JLCPCB library MIT (index vendored). Konnect is AGPL plus commercial and serves as an ideas-only reference. Its dry-run token, lazy toolsets and naming conventions were adopted.
   - Other prior art: KiCAD-MCP-Server, kicad-mcp-pro, the Seeed MCP server, circuit-synth (MIT, no MCP server), atopile, Zener, tscircuit and SKiDL. None publishes a routed board comparable to openMPPT (maintainers' research notes, section 2). The full September survey is in the research notes at `3380b34`.
7. **Sourcing.** One `search_parts` merges results from every registered provider. Assets come from easyeda2kicad (by LCSC ID), the CDFER JLCPCB library, or `kicad_tools.library.generators` (parametric footprints). Datasheets are checked with `pdfplumber` before a part is committed. Part preference: JLCPCB Basic, then Preferred, never Extended for passives.
8. **Library portability.** Assets are written under the project tree, lib tables use `${KIPRJMOD}`-relative URIs, and `check_project_portability` audits them.
9. **Tool conventions.** Names are `snake_case` and verb-first. Units carry a suffix (`_mm`). ID types are disambiguated. Collections take the form `{count, items}` with pagination and a `CHARACTER_LIMIT`. Every tool carries MCP annotations. Errors appear in the result content. Verdicts are evidence-gated: a partial check returns `incomplete`, never a pass. A failed self-check (decision 4) is a demonstrated tool fault and returns `ok: false` with `write_not_verified`, not `incomplete`, which stays the gate's word for missing evidence. `load_toolset` must call `ctx.notify_tools_changed()`, because the SDK does not emit it.

10. **`verify_board` is independent of the router (decided 2026-10-11).** It is built on `kicad-cli` (`pcb drc --refill-zones` plus the unconnected-item report) and returns PASS/FAIL with the complete per-net lists. KRT's own checkers (`check_complete.py`, `board_score.py`, `check_drc.py`, `check_reachability.py`) run only as diagnostics that explain a failure. A disagreement between them and `kicad-cli` is itself a FAIL signal, because KRT parses and writes boards with its own code and would otherwise be grading itself.
11. **What counts as "autonomous pass" (decided 2026-10-11).** A rung is passed autonomously when, with no human intervention, the best of at most N = 5 attempts (varied seeds or router strategies) inside a fixed wall-clock budget per rung passes the gate. The attempt count and the success rate across all attempts are always reported, so a 1-of-5 board is visibly different from a 5-of-5 board. The exact per-rung time budget is set when the rung's ticket is written (starting point: 30 minutes).
    - **Protocol detail (2026-10-11, pivot-ladder ticket 06):** a frozen manifest and attempt plan before the board is seen; every attempt starts from the identical input; levers are only the pre-declared plan's discrete settings (escalation, grid step, max-iterations, ordering strategy; KRT has no seed lever); results are reported per backend and never pooled; any human touch relabels the run "assisted", detected by hash and log; every batch is appended to an index and a rung's result is the first scored batch on that protocol version. Router runtime is unmeasured, so the 30-minute budget is provisional.
12. **Copilot hand-off is file-based (decided 2026-10-11; kept, and the drift guard specified, 2026-10-19, `paradigm-review-2` ticket 11).** The agent prepares a revision (placement, signals, checks) and hands it over through the checkpoint mechanism (decision 4). The human edits power routing and zones in KiCad, saves and closes the file, and the agent reloads, verifies and continues. Live IPC stays out until the file hand-off proves to hurt in practice: kipy 0.8.0 live schematic reads are broken, and the gate and the self-checks judge files. The existing `copilot` IPC toolset stays as it is, but the Copilot hand-off does not depend on it. The drift guard (P1-19) is **not built yet** (build tickets 40 and 41). Its contract:
    - **What is hashed:** one last-known composite hash per project over every file a tool reads or writes (`.kicad_pcb`, `.kicad_pro`, `.kicad_dru`, the `.kicad_sch` files), using the gate field's hashing in `gate_state.py`.
    - **When it changes:** only after a write whose self-check passed (decision 4), or by an explicit reload call. Never otherwise, so an unchecked write can never be re-hashed into looking legitimate (mixelpixx #244).
    - **When it is checked:** before every write, not only in checkpoint pairs. The checkpoint revision token carries this hash (decision 4).
    - **On drift:** the tool refuses with `drift_detected`: the changed files, the known and current hash of each, and the instruction "reload, then re-propose". It never re-proposes by itself, because that would lay the agent's plan over human edits it has not seen. There is no `force` override.
    - **Log:** every drift refusal and every reload is one line in a project log (time, files, hashes).
    - **KiCad open:** while KiCad's lock file for a file exists, writing tools refuse with `file_open_in_kicad`, naming the file. Reads stay allowed. A stale lock file (after a crash) is reported, never deleted automatically.

14. **`verify_board` contract (decided 2026-10-11; pivot-ladder ticket 03 holds the full text; docs/TESTING.md has the gate's contract).**
    - Verdicts are `PASS`, `FAIL` and `INCOMPLETE`, with `FAIL` first. `FAIL` means the board is demonstrably bad; `INCOMPLETE` means the evidence is missing.
    - It grades against the board's own rules plus a pinned baseline of `rule_severities`, `.kicad_dru` `severity` clauses and netclass limits. Any deviation is `INCOMPLETE` until a human re-baselines. There are no waivers.
    - Each verdict is three `kicad-cli pcb drc --refill-zones --severity-all` runs on fresh copies, de-duplicated, with union semantics (any error in any run is `FAIL`).
    - Guards that give `INCOMPLETE`: `kicad-cli` failure, a missing or unannotated schematic, a count at exactly 199 or 499, a rules deviation.
    - It is always loaded (start toolset). Every other tool that returns a board state carries `gate: {verdict, board_sha, age}`, and only `verify_board` may say "routed" or "done". `pcb_run_drc` is rebuilt to include unconnected items.

## Known facts and gotchas

Evidence references are old ticket IDs at `3380b34`, test files, or the maintainers' tickets, cited by number.

**`kicad_tools` (v0.20.x until build ticket 42; v0.23.0 until build ticket 71; v0.24.0 since)**
- **Rechecked on 0.24.0 (build ticket 71).** Every fact below that names 0.23.0 was rerun on 0.23.0 and 0.24.0 side by side (private venv, same dependencies otherwise). Each says fixed, unchanged or changed in 0.24.0. The ones a test can hold are pinned per version in `tests/test_kicad_tools_facts.py` and `tests/test_write_layer.py`.
- **0.23.0's origin rule (build ticket 42).** The origin `PCB.load` applies is the min corner of the bounding box of every top-level Edge.Cuts `gr_*` item, arc bulges and circles included (`core/board_outline.py` `board_outline_bounds`), or (0, 0) with `outline_error` set when an item cannot be read (a `gr_text` on Edge.Cuts, a collinear arc). 0.20.0 used the first `gr_rect` start, else the min over `gr_line` endpoints, which is not the top-left corner of a reversed rect, a polygon or a second rect, so the placement tools refused those boards. `_outline_frame` (`toolsets/placement.py`) now computes 0.23.0's rule from the raw tokens with its own arithmetic (circle through start/mid/end, axis extremes in the swept range), cross-checks the library, and refuses an outline it cannot read exactly (a `gr_curve`, which the library samples, or an item the library cannot read). Proven by `tests/test_placement_frame.py` (raw `(at)` read back; an arc whose bulge sets the origin agrees with the library). **Neither the library nor the raw reader is KiCad's outline box in three more cases, now refused by name (fix round 1):** a footprint carrying `fp_*` items on Edge.Cuts (KiCad's box includes them, both readers ignore them: the RoyalBlue54L NFC-antenna demo's J1 gives KiCad's top 66.560695 against 69.058195 read; `tiny_tapeout`'s J15 is the other demo affected); a `gr_poly` whose `pts` hold an `(arc ...)` (both readers take the xy points only; KiCad loads it); and a legacy `gr_arc` without `(mid ...)` (centre/start/angle form, loads under a KiCad 5 version header): 0.23.0 reads it correctly (it agrees with the arc KiCad's own upgrade writes), the raw reader does not compute it. Origins are rounded to 1e-9 mm on both sides of the cross-check. 0.23.0 also reports segment, via and zone coordinates board-relative, like pads (its `_detect_board_origin` docstring); `route_delta` already added the origin back to both. **Unchanged in 0.24.0:** `core/board_outline.py` is byte-identical and `_detect_board_origin`'s docstring is the same (build ticket 71).
- **Coordinate frames.** `Pad.position`, `Footprint.position` and `Zone.polygon` can be off by the board/page origin (openMPPT, ticket 20). `PCB.add_trace` and `add_via` expect board-local coordinates, while DSN, SES and KRT output use page-absolute ones. `place_footprints` was also reported to mishandle the origin (ticket 20), but that case was **not isolated**. **Root cause of P4-11 (build ticket 43, on 6 KiCad demos against `kicad-cli` pos and IPC-D-356 exports and pcbnew):** in 0.23.0, `Footprint.position` and `Zone.polygon` are board-local, the page position minus `_board_origin` (the min corner of the top-level Edge.Cuts box), neither page nor aux-origin coordinates; with the origin added back the error is 0.000000 mm (pic_programmer: origin (73.66, 40.64), up to 84.13 mm off before). `Pad.position` is the footprint-local raw `(at)` x, y (unrotated, mirrored on the back), 190 to 385 mm off; only origin + footprint position + R(angle)·`Pad.position` matches IPC-D-356. The sanctioned source is now `src/kicad_mcp/raw_geometry.py` (page frame, raw tokens, proven per demo board against KiCad). Further facts from that ticket: a pad's own `(shape)`/`(size)` is KiCad's F.Cu entry even on a back-side footprint, and a KiCad 10 `(padstack (mode front_inner_back|custom) (layer ...))` holds the other copper layers (`PadGeometry.copper_on`); footprint zones are stored in page coordinates; IPC-D-356 positions are relative to the aux origin; KiCad writes a zone's holes as further `(polygon)` nodes. Migrating the toolsets to it is ticket 44.
- **Lost writes.** `Pad.__setattr__` mirrors only `rotation` to the S-expression, so `size`, `drill` and `layers` are lost on `save()`. The fix is to mutate the `(size W H)` node; the pad must have been loaded via `PCB.load()`. `src/` was audited for this pattern: 0 assignments (`paradigm-review-2` tickets 07 and 08; a ratchet ban is build ticket 29) (P3-13). **Fixed in 0.24.0** (#7479, PRs #7499 and #7524): `pad.size = (2.345, 1.234)` reaches the saved tree (raw `(size 2.345 1.234)` read back; 0.23.0 kept `(size 1.6 1.6)`), and `PCB.save` raises `ValueError` naming each edit it still cannot persist (`C3/pad 1.number`) and writes nothing (`tests/test_kicad_tools_facts.py`). The ratchet ban stays: our mutations go through raw nodes and the write layer, not the setters. Likewise, `SymbolInstance.to_sexp()` builds a *detached* tree, so mutating its result does nothing. **Always confirm a mutation by reloading the saved file.**
- **Hierarchical schematics.** Resaving a subsheet through the high-level `Schematic` collapses every symbol's instance path, and the netlist then silently loses components. `schematic.py` has **no guard** (P2-07, high priority). `schema.Schematic.add_power()` omits `(instances ...)` unless `project_name`/`instance_path` are passed. `embed_lib_symbol()` drops `(power global)`, `pin_numbers` and related fields, so the power symbol never joins its net.
- **`PCB.from_schematic()`** without a resolvable `kicad-cli` falls back to a Python netlist extractor, and that extractor once merged VCC and GND onto one net (a silent short). On Windows, `kicad-cli` discovery is missing at six call sites (ERC, annotation, symbol search, gerbers, footprint libs, netlist cross-check). - **Parsing gaps.** Slotted pads `(drill oval W H)` parse as drill 0, so slots disappear from obstacle models (openMPPT L4). `BoardGraphic` parses neither `gr_poly` nor a graphic's `(net ...)`. **KiCadRoutingTools**
- MIT. The CLI has no `pcbnew` dependency and ships a prebuilt Rust binary (deps: numpy, scipy, shapely, Pillow). It never mutates its input. It writes page-absolute coordinates. The core is single-threaded, and its MPS "rounds" are not a safe parallel boundary (P4-08).
- **Rule relaxation.** `--escalation fab` reaches full connectivity by going below the board's floors and rewriting the sibling `.kicad_pro`, so KiCad's own DRC then grades the board clean. Even with `--no-fix-drc-settings`, v0.23.1 still lowers the output project's rules (`min_hole_clearance`, the Default class): KRT #1285, fixed on KRT `main` after v0.23.1, unreleased (the earlier citation of #650 was wrong: #650 is a plane-finalize issue). Until a release carries the fix, keep restoring the original `.kicad_pro` before grading (build ticket 48).
- **Clearance floor.** `board` policy does not bound clearance when `rules.min_clearance = 0.0`, which is what KiCad writes by default. Its rescue pass also ignores `--clearance`. Only `--fab-overrides` holds the floor (golden board: 10 clearance violations dropped to 0).
- **Missing project file.** Without a sibling `.kicad_pro`, KRT uses the STOCK netclass. On openMPPT that produced 18 clearance violations on `Power_20A` (P4-09).
- **Net-ID collision.** `extract_nets()` synthesises IDs on KiCad 10 boards, so `--nets X` hits another net's pads and produces real `shorting_items` (openMPPT: 6 shorts dropped to 0 once fixed). ~~The fix lives only on the local branch `kicad-mcp-local-fixes`.~~ **Fixed upstream** by another contributor in KRT PR #1028 (commit `0a9a2fea`, first released in v0.23.0): `extract_nets()` now picks the net format from the file's content, not its version stamp. Checked on 2026-10-19 with an in-memory repro (wrong pads on v0.21.5 and v0.22.1, correct on v0.23.1; `paradigm-review-2` ticket 01). Our own PR is no longer needed (P4-07).
- **Zone blindness.** Filled copper zones are not obstacles for foreign nets (obstacle map; reproduced in `tests/fixtures/zone_obstacle/`). `propose_route_with_retry` refuses to route a board with filled zones unless `acknowledge_unmodeled_zone_risk=True`. Freerouting is just as blind (P4-06). Re-checked on v0.23.1 (build ticket 48): KRT still routes across a foreign pour by design (a refill carves the pour around the track), and KRT #1260 now blocks only cuts that would split a pour. The 0.0 mm clearance errors on `zone_obstacle` appear only when DRC grades the stale fill; with `--refill-zones` there are 0 errors. So the refusal guard may no longer be needed; removing it needs its own ticket with the gate as the judge.
- **High-fanout nets.** Power and GND nets in point-to-point routing turn into endless runs of 130-250 MST edges. Exclude them and pour them (openMPPT tickets 17/18).

**Freerouting**
- Its self-reported score anti-correlates with real DRC (golden board: best self-score gave 2 shorts). Force `-mt 1`. The 2.3.0 CLI has no partial routing and no via-budget flag. The DSN round trip is lossy (zones, custom DRU rules, keepouts). Runtimes range from minutes to hours.

**KiCad / `kicad-cli` 10.0.5**
- **DRC behaviour.** The clearance-violation count differs between runs on the same unchanged file (61, 59, 64); shorts and unconnected counts were stable. `lib_footprint_mismatch` depends on the installed stock libraries, not on the board. Exclusions do not appear in JSON. Zones are filled with `kicad-cli pcb drc --refill-zones --save-board`.
- **What the DRC report hides** (research, 2026-10-11; pivot-ladder ticket 01). The exit code is 0 with violations unless `--exit-code-violations` is passed. A `.kicad_dru` rule with `(severity ignore)` silences violations with no trace in the report. A missing or unannotated `.kicad_sch` disables `--schematic-parity` and still exits 0. Each violation code is capped at 199 (499 for `clearance` and `unconnected_items`) with no truncation flag. The JSON has no per-net field (parse the bracketed net token, or resolve uuid + position against the board), and descriptions are localized, so key on `type`. `verify_board` must check each of these itself.
- **Rule storage.** Editing only a netclass leaves `rules.min_clearance = 0.0` (meaning unset), so rules must be set in both the netclass and the constraints.
- **SVG export** needs `--mode-multi` and a non-empty `--layers`.
- **IPC API.** It covers the PCB editor only, gets no socket env vars when launched externally, and is single-threaded. SWIG `pcbnew` is deprecated and slated for removal in KiCad 11. kipy 0.8.0's `schematic.py` imports `PageSettings`, which that release does not define, so live schematic reads fail.
- **Fine-pitch rules.** A 0.4 mm-pitch VQFN cannot take a 0.2 mm track with 0.2 mm clearance between its pads. That is why the v1 golden board cannot be routed under its own rules.

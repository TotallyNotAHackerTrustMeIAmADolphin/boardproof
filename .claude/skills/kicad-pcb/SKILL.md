# PCB placement and routing workflow

**Geometry over vision, and never trust an autorouter's output.** Structured collision/DRC queries are the authoritative signal; a rendered image is a human aid only. Both rules exist because this project got burned ignoring them.

1. **Place in batches, then check the geometry.** `place_footprints` adds *and* moves a whole batch in one call (a spec with a `library_id` adds; a spec whose reference already exists moves). `align_footprints` / `distribute_footprints` / `snap_footprints_to_grid` / `pack_footprints_densely` operate on lists of references — never loop a single-footprint call. Coordinates are BOARD-LOCAL millimetres: local (0,0) is the top-left corner of the bounding box of the Edge.Cuts outline (arc bulges and circles included); the tool refuses an outline it cannot read exactly (a curve, an arc inside a polygon, a legacy arc without a mid point, Edge.Cuts graphics inside a footprint, a malformed or unsupported Edge.Cuts item); without any outline coordinates are page coordinates. `place_footprints`, `snap_footprints_to_grid` and `propose_autoplacement` return `origin_mm`, `origin_rule` and `frame` (read from the raw Edge.Cuts nodes) and refuse a board whose outline they cannot read exactly; `align_footprints`, `distribute_footprints` and `confirm_autoplacement` take no coordinates and report local positions. Snapping anchors its grid at the outline corner, not the editor's absolute grid. `place_footprints` keeps an existing footprint's rotation when a spec omits `rotation`. After any placement change, ask the geometry, don't look at a picture: `pcb_check_collisions` for "did I put two things on top of each other", `pcb_validate_proposed_placements` to test an entire placement plan *before* committing it.

2. **Know which check you just ran.** These three are not interchangeable, and conflating them has already caused a real bug here (a placement-only checker was once exposed under the name `pcb_run_drc`):
   - `pcb_check_collisions` — component-vs-component overlap only. Deliberately excludes single-footprint-vs-board constraints, so a board can come back with **0 collisions** and still fail placement: the golden ATtiny1616 board does exactly that, reporting 0 collisions but an `edge_connector_placement` warning on J1.
   - `pcb_check_placement` — the broader pure-Python placement checker (pad clearance, courtyard, edge, hole-to-hole, silk-over-pad, off-board). Fast, good right after moving parts. **Still not a DRC.**
   - `pcb_run_drc` — one real `kicad-cli` DRC run. It is NOT the acceptance gate (`verify_board` is); it says so in its `not_the_gate` field. Read `unconnected_count` as well as `violation_count`: the golden board reports `violation_count: 0` while being completely unrouted, with 11 unconnected items sitting right beside that zero.

3. **Autorouting is a checkpoint, not a step.** `propose_route_with_retry` returns a `revision_token` and writes nothing; `confirm_route` applies it and immediately re-runs the real DRC, returning `drc_after`. **Read that block. Every time.** Autorouter output has looked completely fine here and been wrong repeatedly: Freerouting's own multi-threaded optimizer is documented-broken and generates clearance violations (we force `-mt 1`); its SES output nests net names in a shape `kicad_tools`' importer silently dropped, so a "successful" route applied *zero* traces; coordinates that skipped the `board_origin` offset produced traces sitting millimetres off their pads; and a corrupted-reference-designator bug in `kicad_tools`' DSN export once aliased several nets onto the same pin token, which Freerouting then dutifully routed into real shorts that looked like an autorouting-quality problem and weren't. `traces_applied > 0` is not success. A clean `drc_after` is necessary but still not "done" (see step 7). **Never trust either backend's own self-reported score for ranking a retry** — Freerouting's log and KiCadRoutingTools' `JSON_SUMMARY` have both been observed disagreeing with real DRC on the same board (a better-scoring config with more real shorts than a worse-scoring one); `propose_route_with_retry` always re-grades every attempt with real `kicad-cli` DRC before picking a winner, and so should you if you're comparing runs by hand.

3a. **Two routing backends, one checkpoint.** `propose_route_with_retry(backend=...)` takes `"freerouting"` (default, DSN/SES round-trip, GPLv3 subprocess) or `"kicad_routing_tools"` (MIT, ~50x faster on measurement, reads/writes `.kicad_pcb` directly with no DSN/SES). Its `escalation` param controls how far a failing net may be narrowed below its requested size: `"board"` (default) stays within the board's own declared rules; `"fab"` will go below them to reach full connectivity and must be treated as an explicit, disclosed choice — when used, the proposal includes a `pcb_check_manufacturability`-style verdict, and a `meets_fab_floor: false` result there is not a routing failure, it's the router telling you what it had to relax. Never silently prefer `fab` for a "cleaner" result; a board that's DRC-clean only because its own rules got rewritten underneath it is the exact trap the acceptance gate exists to catch. KiCadRoutingTools runs with `--strict-sizes`: when it delivers a feature below its requested size it exits 3, and the result carries `strict_sizes_violated`. In the MCP tools (`propose_route_with_retry`, `confirm_route`) nothing ever clears that flag: every exit 3 puts `strict_sizes_violated` into the proposal's router flags, the proposal says `requires_acknowledgement`, and `confirm_route` refuses until you pass `acknowledge_router_flags=True` knowingly. Only the rung runner and `autonomy_run` (`rung_backends.with_under_width_check`) clear a stale flag, and only when the raw check of the delivered board's `(segment)`, `(arc)` and `(via)` nodes finds no under-width copper; they keep the router's claim as `strict_sizes_reported_by_router`. Either way KRT results never count as routed without a `verify_board` PASS. KiCadRoutingTools does not treat filled zones as obstacles for other nets, and high-fanout power/GND nets never finish as point-to-point routes. Route the signal nets with power and GND excluded, then pour the zones (docs/ARCHITECTURE.md, "Known facts and gotchas").

4. **`get_unrouted_nets` is a convenience, real `kicad-cli` DRC (and ultimately `verify_board`) is the truth.** The ratsnest query is the right tool for "what's left to route" and for planning, but `kicad_tools`' internal connectivity computation has been observed still calling nets unrouted after a route that real `kicad-cli` DRC reports as fully connected. Where they disagree, DRC wins, and the `gate` field on the output shows the last `verify_board` verdict for that exact file.

5. **Autoplacement is a proposal from a naive heuristic.** `propose_autoplacement` is row-packing by courtyard size — not a solver — and `confirm_autoplacement` consumes the token exactly once. Per docs/ARCHITECTURE.md's human-in-the-loop policy, surface the proposal for review and re-check the applied result with `pcb_check_collisions` / `pcb_check_placement`; don't confirm your own proposal and call the layout done.

6. **Rendering is secondary.** `render_board_diagnostic` (the `diagnostics` toolset) returns `authoritative: false` deliberately. Produce it when a human wants to look at the board, or to catch things geometry can't (silkscreen legibility, an obviously-wrong-looking layout) — never as the basis for a correctness claim. And never hand-edit the `.kicad_pcb`.

7. **"Routed" and "done" require a `verify_board` PASS** (docs/TESTING.md's acceptance gate). That means zero unconnected items on every net, zero error-level DRC, and all zones filled, read from `kicad-cli` as *complete* lists. `verify_board` is always available; every board-state output (placement, routing, DRC, project open) carries `gate: {verdict, board_sha, project_sha, verdict_board_sha, age_s, stale, note}` for exactly that file, and `stale: true` after any mutation or when no verdict exists. No tool but the gate says a board is routed or done; if `gate.verdict` is not `PASS` with `stale: false`, make no such claim and report the full per-net unconnected list and the full violation list instead. "0 shorts", a category count, a sample, or a `kicad_tools` position or ratsnest value has already produced one false "done" here (openMPPT, 2026-09-14). The Copilot tier is the default: on boards above the autonomous ceiling, the human owns power routing and zone topology.

8. **Read the label "PASS (necked)" exactly.** `verify_board` itself returns only PASS, FAIL or INCOMPLETE. The rung runner (protocol 6) adds the label `PASS (necked)` to an attempt whose board passed the gate but carries counted, forced necks: tracks narrower than their net class at a pad entry, where the pad geometry limits the width (the protocol's test is `w > s + 2(g - c)`, which gives 0.2 mm at U1 pin 4 on rung 1), within 2.0 mm of the pad and not below the board's `min_track_width`. It is a weaker statement than a plain PASS. Quote the label with the neck count and length (`necked_count`, `necked_length_mm`) whenever you report such a result, and never shorten it to "PASS" or "routed". No rung is claimed from it. The owner's decision on a neck below the pad limit is still open, and the rung-1 Freerouting result (5 of 5 `PASS (necked)`) depends on it: the first segment of each neck is 0.15 mm wide where the pad would allow 0.2 mm, so a stricter reading would FAIL all five.

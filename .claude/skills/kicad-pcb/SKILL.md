# PCB placement and routing workflow

**Geometry over vision, and never trust an autorouter's output.** Structured collision/DRC queries are the authoritative signal; a rendered image is a human aid only. Both rules exist because this project got burned ignoring them.

1. **Place in batches, then check the geometry.** `place_footprints` adds *and* moves a whole batch in one call (a spec with a `library_id` adds; a spec whose reference already exists moves). `align_footprints` / `distribute_footprints` / `snap_footprints_to_grid` / `pack_footprints_densely` operate on lists of references — never loop a single-footprint call. Coordinates are BOARD-LOCAL millimetres: local (0,0) is the top-left corner of the bounding box of the Edge.Cuts outline (arc bulges and circles included); the tool refuses an outline it cannot read exactly (a curve, an arc inside a polygon, a legacy arc without a mid point, Edge.Cuts graphics inside a footprint, a malformed or unsupported Edge.Cuts item); without any outline coordinates are page coordinates. `place_footprints`, `snap_footprints_to_grid` and `propose_autoplacement` return `origin_mm`, `origin_rule` and `frame` (read from the raw Edge.Cuts nodes) and refuse a board whose outline they cannot read exactly; `align_footprints`, `distribute_footprints` and `confirm_autoplacement` take no coordinates and report local positions. Snapping anchors its grid at the outline corner, not the editor's absolute grid. `snap_footprints_to_grid` takes an optional `anchor` ("outline", the default, or "absolute"): "absolute" snaps the page coordinate to a grid whose origin is the grid origin KiCad stores in the board, `(setup (grid_origin x y))` (page (0, 0) when none), and refuses a malformed grid_origin or any other value, writing nothing. `place_footprints` keeps an existing footprint's rotation when a spec omits `rotation`. After any placement change, ask the geometry, don't look at a picture: `pcb_check_collisions` for "did I put two things on top of each other", `pcb_validate_proposed_placements` to test an entire placement plan *before* committing it.

1a. **A move reports the collisions it causes (issue 264).** `place_footprints`, `pack_footprints_densely`, `align_footprints`, `distribute_footprints`, `snap_footprints_to_grid`, `place_footprints_at_edge`, `propose_move_group` / `confirm_move_group` and `propose_placement_optimization` / `confirm_placement_optimization` list the courtyard overlaps (hull of courtyard and pads) in which a footprint they moved takes part: `collision_check` (`measured` or `not_measured`), `collisions` (each `between` two references and `existed_before`), `collisions_count` and `collisions_truncated` (the first 20; `detail=true` lists all, and a partial or refused answer always does) and `collisions_not_measured` (footprints with neither courtyard nor pad, absent from the check). It is a measurement of moved footprints only; a move into a collision stays allowed. `collisions_count` 0 says only that none was measured, and with `not_measured` there is no count: never read either as no collision. Run `pcb_check_collisions` for the whole board.

2. **Know which check you just ran.** These three are not interchangeable, and conflating them has already caused a real bug here (a placement-only checker was once exposed under the name `pcb_run_drc`):
   - `pcb_check_collisions` — component-vs-component overlap only. Deliberately excludes single-footprint-vs-board constraints, so a board can come back with **0 collisions** and still fail placement: the golden ATtiny1616 board does exactly that, reporting 0 collisions but an `edge_connector_placement` warning on J1.
   - `pcb_check_placement` — the broader pure-Python placement checker (pad clearance, courtyard, edge, hole-to-hole, silk-over-pad, off-board). Fast, good right after moving parts. **Still not a DRC.**
   - `pcb_run_drc` — one real `kicad-cli` DRC run. It is NOT the acceptance gate (`verify_board` is); it says so in its `not_the_gate` field. Read `unconnected_count` as well as `violation_count`: the golden board reports `violation_count: 0` while being completely unrouted, with 11 unconnected items sitting right beside that zero.

3. **Autorouting is a checkpoint, not a step.** `propose_route_with_retry` returns a `revision_token` and writes nothing; `confirm_route` applies it and immediately re-runs the real DRC, returning `drc_after`. **Read that block. Every time.** Autorouter output has looked completely fine here and been wrong repeatedly: Freerouting's own multi-threaded optimizer is documented-broken and generates clearance violations (we force `-mt 1`); its SES output nests net names in a shape `kicad_tools`' importer silently dropped, so a "successful" route applied *zero* traces; coordinates that skipped the `board_origin` offset produced traces sitting millimetres off their pads; and a corrupted-reference-designator bug in `kicad_tools`' DSN export once aliased several nets onto the same pin token, which Freerouting then dutifully routed into real shorts that looked like an autorouting-quality problem and weren't. `traces_applied > 0` is not success. A clean `drc_after` is necessary but still not "done" (see step 7). **Never trust either backend's own self-reported score for ranking a retry** — Freerouting's log and KiCadRoutingTools' `JSON_SUMMARY` have both been observed disagreeing with real DRC on the same board (a better-scoring config with more real shorts than a worse-scoring one); `propose_route_with_retry` always re-grades every attempt with real `kicad-cli` DRC before picking a winner, and so should you if you're comparing runs by hand.

3a. **Two routing backends, one checkpoint.** `propose_route_with_retry(backend=...)` takes `"freerouting"` (default, DSN/SES round-trip, GPLv3 subprocess) or `"kicad_routing_tools"` (MIT, ~50x faster on measurement, reads/writes `.kicad_pcb` directly with no DSN/SES). Its `escalation` param controls how far a failing net may be narrowed below its requested size: `"board"` (default) stays within the board's own declared rules; `"fab"` will go below them to reach full connectivity and must be treated as an explicit, disclosed choice — when used, the proposal includes a `pcb_check_manufacturability`-style verdict, and a `meets_fab_floor: false` result there is not a routing failure, it's the router telling you what it had to relax. Never silently prefer `fab` for a "cleaner" result; a board that's DRC-clean only because its own rules got rewritten underneath it is the exact trap the acceptance gate exists to catch. KiCadRoutingTools runs with `--strict-sizes`: when it delivers a feature below its requested size it exits 3, and the result carries `strict_sizes_violated`. In the MCP tools (`propose_route_with_retry`, `confirm_route`) nothing ever clears that flag: every exit 3 puts `strict_sizes_violated` into the proposal's router flags, the proposal says `requires_acknowledgement`, and `confirm_route` refuses until you pass `acknowledge_router_flags=True` knowingly. Only the rung runner and `autonomy_run` (`rung_backends.with_under_width_check`) clear a stale flag, and only when the raw check of the delivered board's `(segment)`, `(arc)` and `(via)` nodes finds no under-width copper; they keep the router's claim as `strict_sizes_reported_by_router`. Either way KRT results never count as routed without a `verify_board` PASS. KiCadRoutingTools does not treat filled zones as obstacles for other nets, and high-fanout power/GND nets never finish as point-to-point routes. Route the signal nets with power and GND excluded, then pour the zones (docs/ARCHITECTURE.md, "Known facts and gotchas").

4. **`get_unrouted_nets` is a convenience, real `kicad-cli` DRC (and ultimately `verify_board`) is the truth.** The ratsnest query is the right tool for "what's left to route" and for planning, but `kicad_tools`' internal connectivity computation has been observed still calling nets unrouted after a route that real `kicad-cli` DRC reports as fully connected. Where they disagree, DRC wins, and the `gate` field on the output shows the last `verify_board` verdict for that exact file.

5. **Autoplacement is a proposal from a naive heuristic.** `propose_autoplacement` is first-fit row packing by courtyard size — not a solver — and `confirm_autoplacement` consumes the token exactly once. With an outline that is one axis-aligned rectangle (its corners may be rounded by quarter arcs; rounded corners and slots are obstacles) every proposed courtyard lies inside it (`edge_clearance_mm`, default 0.5, from the edge; `spacing_mm`, default 2, from other courtyards), footprints you did not name and locked ones are obstacles, and parts that fit nowhere refuse the proposal as `does_not_fit`; any other outline is refused as `outline_not_rectangular`. Rotations are kept. Per docs/ARCHITECTURE.md's human-in-the-loop policy, surface the proposal for review and re-check the applied result with `pcb_check_collisions` / `pcb_check_placement`; don't confirm your own proposal and call the layout done.

5a. **Edges and locks.** `place_footprints_at_edge` puts a batch of existing footprints `offset_mm` in from a `left`/`right`/`top`/`bottom` edge of the outline (optionally `along_mm` along it, from the top-left corner); rotate a part first with `place_footprints`, the tool keeps its rotation. It measures the courtyard from the raw footprint tokens, refuses the whole call (nothing written) for an unknown or locked footprint, a courtyard that would leave the outline or overlap another one, and reads the positions back from the reloaded file. `lock_footprints` sets or clears the raw `(locked yes)` token; `place_footprints`, `align_footprints`, `distribute_footprints`, `snap_footprints_to_grid`, `pack_footprints_densely` and `propose_autoplacement` never move a locked footprint (`locked_skipped`, or a refusal), and `confirm_autoplacement` refuses a proposal for one locked since. `propose_placement_optimization` leaves locked footprints out of its proposal (`locked_skipped`) and `confirm_placement_optimization` refuses a proposal for one locked since. Groups by schematic sheet are not built yet. `place_footprints`, `lock_footprints`, `propose_board_outline` and `confirm_board_outline` answer with counts and at most 20 items of an attention list; pass `detail=true` for every item (a partial or error `place_footprints` answer lists `placed` and `moved` anyway). `align_footprints`, `distribute_footprints`, `snap_footprints_to_grid` and `place_footprints_at_edge` list the first 20 `moved` with `moved_count`; `detail=true` lists all, `locked_skipped` is never cut.

5b. **Keepouts and isolation slots.** `propose_keepouts_and_slots` / `confirm_keepouts_and_slots` add or remove, in one batch and on a NEW board revision, keepout zones (`keepouts: [{name, rect | polygon, layers, rules}]`: tracks, vias, pads, copperpour, footprints `allowed` or `not_allowed` per copper layer; the default forbids tracks, vias and copper pour) and slots (`slots: [{x1, y1, x2, y2, width_mm, ends}]`, a closed cut-out inside the outline in `Edge.Cuts`, for example a milled slot between a mains side and a low-voltage side; `round` ends are what a milling bit cuts). Page mm. kicad-cli DRC does not see a track, via, pad or part that lies wholly inside a slot, so the proposal looks for them itself and refuses the whole batch, naming them; there is no override, and a slot over or around an item is a conflict at any margin, 0 included. A slot that would reach the outline is refused (that changes the outline: `propose_board_outline`, which in turn refuses a board that has slots and lists them). Removing a slot (by the id the tool lists) or a keepout (by name) is a loosening, possible only by naming it in a proposal. A copper pour under either is listed as `zone_overlaps` and needs a refill. The confirm checks the reload against the whole plan and that DRC reports no new outline, board-edge or keepout error; run `verify_board` for the verdict. Placement tools treat slots as missing material (`in_cutout`, packing around them), and `place_footprints` refuses a move of an existing footprint into one for the whole call (`in_cutout`; also `outline_arcs_unreadable` for an outline whose arcs are not all quarter-circle corners of a rectangle (so an outer loop of lines and arcs, or a gr_poly, that is neither a plain rectangle nor a rectangle with quarter-circle corner arcs is refused for the whole call, nothing written (outline_arcs_unreadable when it has any arc, such as a bite, a notch or a corner built from several arcs; outline_not_rectangular when it is lines only or a gr_poly, such as an L shape); circles, stadiums and well-formed slots are not refused; any other Edge.Cuts problem (a stray or open item, a slot that touches the outline or another slot, a duplicate outer loop, an item the tool cannot read) is refused as slots_unreadable; a board with no Edge.Cuts at all is not checked), `slots_unreadable`, and `footprint_unmeasurable` for a named footprint with neither courtyard nor pad). The fab's own minimum slot width is not checked; ask `get_manufacturer_design_rules` and the fab.

5c. **Is each decoupling capacitor next to its supply pin? `check_decoupling_placement(pcb_path)` (verification toolset), one call for the whole board.** For every capacitor (`C<digits>`) with a pad on a supply net and every IC (`U<digits>`) pad on the same net it reports `gap_mm` (capacitor supply pad edge to IC pad edge, the measure of the rung-1 placement rule of build ticket 114, from raw pad tokens), `path_estimate_mm`, `same_side`, and flags a capacitor `too_far` when its nearest same-side supply pin is over `max_pad_gap_mm` (default 1.5) away, `different_side` when none is on its side. **Read `classification` before the rows**: supply nets are found by name (voltage names such as `+3V3`, `VCC`, `VDD`, `VBUS`; `power_nets` adds your own, `use_name_patterns: false` turns the patterns off), ground nets are never supply, a net a capacitor and an IC share that is neither is listed in `unclassified_shared_nets` and NOT checked, and a supply pin is any IC pad on a supply net because the board file has no pin type. **What it is not:** not a verdict on layout quality (a board can pass it and be a bad layout, or fail it with a good one), not `verify_board`, not a route, inductance, via or return-path check, not a datasheet rule, and it does not know how many capacitors a pin needs. There is no pass word: `summary.all_checked_within_bound` true means only that every checked capacitor supply pad met the bound, and it must be read with `summary.coverage_complete`, which is false whenever nothing was checked, something is named in `skipped` (a duplicate or empty reference, a capacitor with no supply pin) a net is left in `unclassified_shared_nets` or any footprint on a supply net was left out by the reference rule (`excluded_footprints_on_supply_nets`: test points, connectors and diodes make this common, by intent, because complete means nothing on a supply net was left unjudged); `classification.excluded_by_reference_rule_on_supply_nets` names every non-`C`/`U` footprint on a supply net, and a voltage name with a suffix (`3V3_EN`) counts as a supply net. A roundrect pad is measured by its bounding rectangle (`pad_boxes: bounding`). Fix a flagged capacitor with `place_footprints`, then run the check again.

6. **Rendering is secondary.** `render_board_diagnostic` (the `diagnostics` toolset) returns `authoritative: false` deliberately. Produce it when a human wants to look at the board, or to catch things geometry can't (silkscreen legibility, an obviously-wrong-looking layout) — never as the basis for a correctness claim. And never hand-edit the `.kicad_pcb`.

7. **"Routed" and "done" require a `verify_board` PASS** (docs/TESTING.md's acceptance gate). That means zero unconnected items on every net, zero error-level DRC, and all zones filled, read from `kicad-cli` as *complete* lists. `verify_board` is always available; every board-state output (placement, routing, DRC, project open) carries `gate: {verdict, board_sha, project_sha, verdict_board_sha, age_s, stale, note}` for exactly that file, and `stale: true` after any mutation or when no verdict exists. No tool but the gate says a board is routed or done; if `gate.verdict` is not `PASS` with `stale: false`, make no such claim and report the full per-net unconnected list and the full violation list instead. "0 shorts", a category count, a sample, or a `kicad_tools` position or ratsnest value has already produced one false "done" here (openMPPT, 2026-09-14). The Copilot tier is the default: on boards above the autonomous ceiling, the human owns power routing and zone topology.

8. **Read the label "PASS (necked)" exactly.** `verify_board` itself returns only PASS, FAIL or INCOMPLETE. The rung runner (protocol 6) adds the label `PASS (necked)` to an attempt whose board passed the gate but carries counted, forced necks: tracks narrower than their net class at a pad entry, where the pad geometry limits the width (the protocol's test is `w > s + 2(g - c)`, which gives 0.2 mm at U1 pin 4 on rung 1), within 2.0 mm of the pad and not below the board's `min_track_width`. It is a weaker statement than a plain PASS. Quote the label with the neck count and length (`necked_count`, `necked_length_mm`) whenever you report such a result, and never shorten it to "PASS" or "routed". No rung is claimed from it. The owner's decision on a neck below the pad limit is still open, and the rung-1 Freerouting result (5 of 5 `PASS (necked)`) depends on it: the first segment of each neck is 0.15 mm wide where the pad would allow 0.2 mm, so a stricter reading would FAIL all five.
